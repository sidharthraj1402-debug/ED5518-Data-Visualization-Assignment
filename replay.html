Where work waits, and why the slowest station keeps changing
Submission: Sidharth Raj M · NS26Z087 Data: 21 Sep – 2 Oct 2026 · day shift 08:00–14:00, evening shift 14:00–20:00 · all times IST · made-up case and data
The line: four stations in a row, ST10 press → ST20 screw-tightening → ST30 leak test → ST40 packing. Between them are three small shelves where units wait: B12, B23 and B34. The whole line can only go as fast as its slowest station.

In one sentence
The dashboard blames ST10 (press) in every shift, but ST10 is only waiting for slower stations, and since 28 Sep the real problem has been ST30 (leak test), where almost half the units with the new SL-27 seals have to be tested twice. High confidence
What I found
The dashboard (PulseBoard) puts ST10 at the top of its "loss minutes" list in all 20 shifts. Those minutes are just ST10 waiting for the stations after it, so they say nothing bad about ST10 itself. The slowest station, the one that sets the pace for the whole line, changed over the two weeks:
  • 21–25 Sep: ST20 (screw-tightening) was the slowest.
  • 24, 25 and 30 Sep, 10:00–13:00: ST20 got even slower on H-type units, 59 s each instead of 33 s.
  • From 28 Sep: ST30 (leak test) became the slowest, after a new batch of seals (SL-27) arrived.
High: ST10 is not the problem High: ST30 is the slowest now
The question nobody asked
Everyone was asking: which station has the most loss minutes? The better question is: which station is slowest while it's actually working, and what is taking up its time?

The dashboard only counts the time a station is blocked or stopped. So a station that works the whole shift, but slowly, gets a score of zero.
What I'd do
  • Work on ST30 (leak test) first, by changing how many SL-27 units go to it. No new equipment yet.
  • Spend nothing on ST10 (press). It isn't the problem.
  • Next evening shift, if Quality agrees: use SL-27 seals on only 1 unit in 10, and check whether shelf B23 stops filling up and output goes up.
  • Day shift: check the ST20 screwdriver bit before 10:00.
  • Any shift: if shelf B34 fills up while ST40 (packing) is stopped, go to ST40 first.
Full plan, limits and who does what
Medium: fewer SL-27 units will fix it Low: why ST20 slows on H-type units

    Decision

    Which single station should get the effort first?

    Priya asked for one station. My answer is ST30 (leak test), starting with a change in how it's used rather than buying new equipment. Since the SL-27 seals arrived on 28 Sep, almost half of those units fail their first leak test and have to go through the same tester again. That costs about 100 units every evening shift, which makes it the biggest loss on the line right now. Quality has only released SL-27 conditionally while they talk to the supplier. If SL-27 goes away, ST20 becomes the slowest station again and a second tester would sit idle, so I'd rather find out how long SL-27 is staying before anyone spends money.

    I wouldn't spend anything on ST10 (press). It's the fastest station on the line, at about 27 seconds a unit in every shift, and its "loss minutes" are just time spent waiting for the stations after it. The temperature alarm screenshots going round didn't match any drop in output either.

    Why a replay is better than an email

    An email could just say "look at ST30". What it can't show is the order things happen in: which station stops first, and which shelf fills first. ST10's "loss minutes" go up in three of these episodes, each time for a different reason, and the shelves are what tell them apart.

    Limits of the data, and how I handled them

    • Two clocks. Each station event has time (the machine's own clock) and received_at (when the server logged it, 0–3 seconds later). I used time everywhere.
    • Duplicates. The station file had 78 rows sent twice by the network (54,521 rows, but 54,443 real events). I removed the copies before building this page.
    • How long a state lasts. A station stays in a state until its next event. I cut the last state of each shift off at the shift end (14:00 or 20:00). With that, every station adds up to exactly 360 minutes in all 80 station-shifts, which is a good check that nothing is missing.
    • Shelves are counted once a minute. So I haven't claimed anything finer than a minute from them, and the replay shows each count for its whole minute without guessing in between.
    • The production system only records when a unit finishes a station, not when it started, and a few scans are missing. The time between finishes shows the pace of the line, which only equals a station's own speed when that station is the slowest. For example, ST20 looks like about 34–36 seconds after 28 Sep because ST30 is holding it back.
    • No reason codes. Holds and operator checks don't say why they happened, so the causes I give for episodes 2 and 4 rely partly on handover notes and the stockroom ledger. Those are people's records, not machine data, and they're marked that way in the replay.
    • One temperature sensor. ST10 has only one, so I can't tell a real heating problem from a sensor problem.
    • Units lost are estimates, measured against the week-1 average of about 653. On 30 Sep (day) and 1 Oct (evening) two problems overlap, so those figures are rough.
    • Names and times that mislead. The roster calls the E shift "Evening/Night", but it runs 14:00–20:00. The ledger's posted time is later than what happened on the floor, so I used the physical handover time.
    • Made-up data. It's consistent, but it hasn't been checked against a real line.
